General tips
--

**Turn up on time!**  Your final vivas are assessments, and we WILL start without you. The MVP and Beta vivas are practice for the final, so should be treated as such.

**Turn up ready to go** - when you walk through the door, you should have the presentation and the demo loaded up and ready to run on at least 2 laptops, so if one person's machine freezes, there is a backup ready.  

**Rehearse beforehand** - you shouldn't be reading off phones and screens.  Everyone in the team should know what's on every slide, so if someone is ill and can't show up on the day, other team mates can take over.


---

While in general the presentations were better than last year's MVP presentations, a surprising number of you hadn't read the brief (find this in the Assessments folder)!  

The brief sets out exactly what we're looking for in the marking - for all units, you should be looking at the project brief and marking criteria and making it really explicit how you've met it. **Assume your markers are stupid, and can't make inferences** - we can only mark what we see, and need to be told why you deserve your marks! Never assume we will infer how you've met the mark scheme - tell us!


Ideal structure
--

The ideal presentation follows this format:

* Introduce yourselves
* Introduce the client (1 or 2 sentences) - who are they/what they do
* Context: 1 sentence on what their business problem is
* What the objective of your project is 1 sentence
* User stories in the [standard user story format](https://github.com/spe-uob/SEP2025/blob/main/Assessments/SEP%202024-5%20formative%20viva%20feedback.md) (As a... I want to... so I can...)
* Product demo - structure around user stories and [flow steps](https://github.com/spe-uob/SEP2025/blob/main/Assessments/SEP%202024-5%20formative%20viva%20feedback.md)
* Tech stack and Architecture diagram - and why you chose them
* Security - very brief
* Code testing/CI/CD - do you have it?  If yes, show us the diagram and tell us what % of code coverage and how many unit tests you have.  if you've been uisng Test-Driven Development, tell us!
* Interface design - why you made the choices
* User testing - how many people have tested it your product and how did it change as a result?  Show us stats and numbers
* Client feedback - and how it's changed as a result
* Project management - how you're managing as a team - do you have a team leader?  show us screenshots of your kanban board
* Team processes - Are you Agile? How many sprints? How many client meetings? how are you doing PRs (show us screenshots!)
* Problems you faced and your solutions (remember to focus on the positives)
* (MVP  Beta: Plan for next phase)
* (Final viva: What you've learned and what you would do differently if you were starting again)

This seems like a LOT in a 10 minute presentation, but the best teams manage it by being fast and snappy, and not reading everything they have on the slides - for example, if you show us the tech stack and architecture diagram, you don't need to talk through what's on there - you can leave the slide up while you talk about why you made the decisions.  Again, if you have 1 slide with multiple user stories, you can tell us one briefly, and move on.  

A lot of you put the major focus on the technical details - which makes sense, you're Computer Scientists! - but remember SEP is about the journey, not the destination, so we're more interested in why you made choices and how you worked together.


Presenting
--

* Never show us the log in screen!  Have your demo loaded up and ready to go - we assume you can make a log in, we never need to see it.

* Try hard not to read off slides/screens, and never read from your phones.  Some of you were good at not reading from phones, but turned your back on the audience to read from slides - don't do this!  If you're worried about your English, remember it's better to have less good English than it is to read off the slides.

* You want to be looking out at the audience, making eye contact with different people - don't turn your back on the audience.

* Please close all the other windows on your laptops before you present! It's distracting, and you might show us things you don't want us to see.  


Demos
--

*  If you are making a phone app, borrow phones from the Tech Hub and show us it loaded up on a phone, so we can play with it during the presentation.
  
* BUT! If you have the demo on the phone, show is a live demo on the screen, too 

* If you're making a web app, show us the web address, so we can look at it. 

* Take screencaps of the product working, and screenshots, as a backup for if something goes wrong.

* Keep a video demo ready to go, in case there are unforeseen problems, but this should be a last resort.  




Project management
--

* Tell us what project management tools you're using - if you're using Agile (and you should be!) how many sprints have you had? 

* Show us screenshots of your kanban board and other tools (Gantt charts/what you communication platform looks like) 

* Tell us what your week looks like - when do you meet? How do you work? 



Slides in general
--

* Start by introducing yourselves - it personalises you and helps us remember who to give marks to! 

* Lots of slides have too many words - aim at bullet points

* We love seeing an iterative design process - so screenshots/photos of original sketches and the different iterations of our projects, and knowing why you changed things 


SEP-specific issues
--
* If you've used any AI tools or tools to generate code, tell us, and tell us why! Make sure you've read and understood the unit AI policy. 

* Equally, if you're using anything that's an off-the-shelf plug in, tell us! It won't lose you marks - we like smart chocies!

* If you're doing something using an API, eg the Open AI API, make sure you tell us how you're training it, and making sure it is specialised to your project.  You need to make it explicit if it is more than just a wrapper around the exisitng API, as that doesn't get many marks. The marking panel will asssume you are just using APIs out of the box, unless you tell us.  

* If you're doing things like Machine Learning/Data Vision/Robotics etc, that we don't teach you, tell us! Emphasise how you have learned it as a team - for example, we will love to hear that team members have taught each other.  But also remember to show us/emphasise the software engineering in your project.  

* Some teams seem to be wanting everything perfect in Figma before starting coding, and this is a problem - your designs SHOULD evolve!  Ideally you should get 1 page prototyped up, with a skeleton of what other pages you'll have, but don't waste time on gorgeous Figma designs of every page, as the designs should be changing and iterating. 


If you're using pre-existing code
--

- Make it really clear what is your work and what you started with – a good way is to show screenshots/screencaps of what you got given, so we can see your work visually - if you can show it side-by-side, that's really strong.  

* If you've spent time refactoring code, or have spent time adding comments etc, show us 'before' and 'after' screenshots
