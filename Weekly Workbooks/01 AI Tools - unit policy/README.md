# AI Unit Policy 

### Overview 

Software Engineering Project (SEP) is about learning the software engineering process, not just producing code. AI coding agents are permitted for writing code, but we strongly advise against using them for the engineering practices the unit exists to teach, including version control, continuous integration, code review, testing, documentation and team communication. In particular, we advise that all software engineering processes are performed manually until at least Christmas (end of TB1). This is a strong recommendation; we will not police the use of AI. The consequence of ignoring it is depriving yourself of learning, not losing marks. In TB2 we will introduce AI coding tools and best practices, so that the engineering skills and judgement you built manually in TB1 will become the foundation for using these tools well. 

### What do we mean by AI? 

Anything built on large language models or generative AI: chat assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), in--editor assistants and autocomplete (e.g., Copilot, Cursor), agentic coding tools that work in your terminal (e.g., Claude Code, Codex), and generative tools for images, diagrams or slides (Midjourney, DALL·E). Note that AI features are increasingly on by default in IDEs, browsers and GitHub, so check what your tools are doing. If you're unsure, please speak to us. 

### Are we allowed to use AI for coding? 

Yes, but you do not have to. If you do use AI-generated code, you still need to understand exactly what it's doing. You must be able to answer detailed, technical questions about it from teammates, clients, and at the viva. Your teammates should absolutely be asking these questions during code review and when resolving merge conflicts. You are ultimately responsible for every line you commit -- if AI-generated code goes wrong, that responsibility is yours. 

### Why is coding allowed but not engineering practices? 

Code generation is where AI is most mature and least harmful to the learning outcomes of this unit; the unit's learning outcomes are about the software engineering process. The engineering practices (e.g., Git, CI, code review, testing, documentation, communication) are what the module aims to teach you. Automating them removes the learning entirely. 

If you are using AI-generated code, your team-mates will have to do code reviews on it. This means you cannot just drop a large piece of generated code into a PR and expect your team-mates to work it out. If this happens, your team-mates will not review it until you have broken it down into smaller, manageable PRs, clearly labelled, commented, in sensible-sized chunks that are clearly defined features. It is your responsibility to make sure team-mates can work with it, not theirs! 

### Will the use of AI be considered during marking? 

The use of AI is not taken into account during marking, in either direction. What we assess is your engineering process: your GitHub history, code reviews, team agreement, and your ability to explain your code and decisions in relation to the software engineering process at the viva. If you've relied heavily on AI for the practices we recommend manually, this tends to become obvious on its own. 

To be explicit: if your team chooses to write everything manually and uses no AI coding tools at all, you will not be marked down for it -- even if this means your final product is smaller in scope or less polished than a team that made heavy use of AI. We mark your engineering process and your understanding, not the sophistication of what AI let you build. A team that manually delivers a smaller system with strong process, deep understanding and good teamwork can score as well as, or better than, a team that used AI heavily to deliver more but can't fully explain or defend it. 

### Never input any of your clients code or business information 

Remember that everything you enter into an AI tool leaves your control immediately and becomes visible to, and usable by, the service provider. If your project includes any proprietary code client code or  data, or information about how they work as a business, do not input this into LLMs -- anything you enter may be used to train models or in other ways you cannot control. 

Most client projects are covered by a contract that explicitly restricts sharing the client's code, data or business information with third parties -- an AI provider counts as a third party. Entering client material into an AI tool can put you in breach of that contract regardless of what the AI provider's own terms say, and this is a legal matter, not just good practice. Check what your contract or agreement with the client actually says, and if you're unsure what it covers, ask your client or your supervisor before using any AI tool on that part of the work.
