SEP MVP and Beta Release presentation feedback - 2023-4
---

Rather than make 2 documents, I've added the Beta Release feedback to the bottom.  This means there is repetition - but that's because a surprising number of groups didn't seem to have read this before the presentations! 

MVP
---
- A surprising number of groups didn’t include the name of their client, or what they did!  Start by telling us this, with an ‘elevator pitch’ about your project goals (Edutopia’s ‘What if Sid Meier’s Civilization could teach you quantum physics?’ was great).

- Don’t be afraid to tell us you started slowly, didn’t work well or could have done more in TB1 – if you can turn that around in TB2 and explain what you learned and what you did differently, it will give you more marks than if you try to pretend everything went smoothly

- But be careful about blaming other deadlines for not doing much work on SEP – every group has the same deadlines (except the Maths & CS students!) so if other groups are doing more than you, this will backfire on you.

- Make sure the slides and product demos are on everyone’s laptops, and you are all ready to jump in if someone has a tech issue.  We understand that tech issues happen – and it looks really smooth and professional when you are pre-prepared to deal with them.  (Do the same when you go out to present to your client).

- Make sure your demos are loaded up on everyone’s laptops before you start, and you have checked that they will work in the building you are presenting in before you start.  

- Make sure that you include everything we’ve asked you for in the pre-viva emails!  For example, some teams didn’t include user stories.


Presenting
--

- Some of you were amazing!  The presentations that worked best had really seamless passing between presenters, and we could tell you'd practiced it. Practicing also means you don’t need to read the information from the screens.  

- Some were equally obvious you’d pulled the presentations together at the last minute and hadn’t practiced them!  Taking half an hour to present to and watch a friendly group, and give each other feedback, will make a big difference – and it’s good practice for future assessments.

- Practicing also means you can keep to time – in the final vivas we WILL stop you if you run over the time slot!  If you feel you’ve got too much to say, focus on the product demo – we will ask you questions if we want more info. 

- When we ask about the workload in the team, it’s always great to hear team-mates promote each others’ work – it will give you more ticks in the ‘team working’ marking boxes.  Don’t lie – if someone is not working as much, we can see – but we were really impressed at how some of the teams supported/promoted each other.  

- Try to sound enthusiastic!  We know that vivas can make you anxious, but treat them like an interview – you want to sound interested in your work.  Again, practice this – it’s usually just a case of adding a bit of energy into your delivery.  It can sound corny, but remember to smile too.


Slides
--

- Have a slide with your tech stack on it.

- Architecture diagrams always make the presentations better.

- Think about the lectures/videos from people who teach you – and steal their best ideas!  Especially in terms of the number of words on the slides.  Don’t be like the team who had just one-word slides, but if in doubt, remove words from slides – always have bullet points, not full sentences. 




Product Demos
--

- Don’t show us the log-in screens!  Start logged in, so we can see it, and only talk about the log in if you want to demonstrate different views for different users.  If we want to know about the log-ins, we’ll ask.


Feedback for groups extending previous projects
--

- Make it really clear what is your work and what is the previous group’s – a good way is to show screenshots of where the previous group finished, so we can see your work visually.

Beta Release
---

Overall - in general, groups really stepped up!  It was espcially good to see that more groups had got more energy in your presentations than the first round - well done!

General presentation skills
--
- Again, we have some excellent presenters out there!  If you know someone in your team is especially good, put them on first!  Ideally all of you should speak - if you're nervous, practice it over and over until it's second nature.  

- A surprising number of groups hadn't included things we specifically asked for in the Viva Brief!  Make sure you read that!
  
- Turn up on time - we will be starting the vivas at the time we tell you, so if your time is 10:00, we expect you to be at the door, ready to strart straight away at bang on 10:00, and won't wait for people. 
  
- Start by introducing each member of the team, as the markers won't all know you.

- Have your slideshow set up on the main laptop and a backup, so when you plug in, it's right there ready to go, and you know it works in the building.
  
- Keep words on slides to a minimum - it's better to have lots of short slides than 1 with lots of words.  There's no such thing as too many slides in a slidedeck!

- Rehearse in front of an audience at least once - ask another team to watch you and give you feedback, as if they are your markers.  Practice your presentation so you keep to time - remember, we WILL stop you when your time is up, so if you haven't talked about something, it won't count.  

- Don't stand in front of the screen - and when you're presenting, try to read from the screen instead of from your laptops, and definitely not from phones! If you need prompts, look and gesture at the screen.  You want to be looking out at the audience, and varying who you're looking at, to keep us feeling engaged.  

- Remember this unit is about the processes and the team working, so keep that front and centre.

- Strong presentations had a nice flow to them:  client intro -> brief -> user story -> product demo -> tech you used and why you made those choices (architecture diagram, tech stack) -> user testing and how it changed the product -> project management and how you worked as a team -> what you learned & what you'd do differently.  

- Spelling errors are distracting, so make sure you proof-read the presentations.
  
- Some of you need to self-promote more.  Think about the best presentations you saw, and emulate them - or think about how you'd promote your best friend, and apply all that to your team.
  
- Don't be afraid to have touches of humour and show your personality.  It's a professional presentation, but we still want you to be yourselves. 
  
- Remember the lesson from the testing day: make sure you don't have other tabs open when you're demonstrating, in case they reveal more about yourself than you want.  Similarly, check your bookmarks and your lockscreen, screensaver and desktop, to make sure they're appropriate images.  

SEP specific presentation issues
--
- Assume your markers have never seen your project, have no idea about your client, etc.  Treat us like we're stupid!
  
- Tell us who your client is and what they do.  Let us know their level of technical understanding - and if they're students, make that really clear, as we know they will be inexperienced clients. Tell us how many times you met with the client and how you worked with them.  Definitely tell us any client management you did.
  
- Please include an architecture diagram!

- If you used Agile methodology tell us!  We want to know about your project management.  Again, assume we're stupid, so need to be told what it means.  Add in screenshots of good kanban boards, and anything you used like gantt charts, how you communicated etc. 
  
- Please include a user story ('as a.... I want to... so I can...') - you don't need to read this out, but have it on a slide so ew can see it while you're explaining the brief

- Tell us what testing you did (ideally include how many people have tested it in diffrent rounds of testing) and how your product changed as a result.  This could be a diagram if you don't want to talk about all of it in detail.  Especially emphasise any testing you did outside of testing day.  

- Tell us what feedback you have had from the client and how the product changd as a result.  
  
- If you have a live website, it's really great to give us the URL so we can click through it.
  
- Show us how your work changed and iterated from MVP to beta to final release - use screenshots and gifs so we can understand the changes and be impressed by the evolution.  If you have initial sketches/diagrams, compare them to the final version. 
  
- If you're showing us videos/gifs of demos, in general slow them down!  Try talking over the top as you record them, to get them to a sensible speed.


Product demos
--
- If you have a mobile app, you can borrow a phone (or a tablet etc) from the Tech Hub to show it off.  It's always much more impressive to show us how it would be used in the wild.  Get your equipment early, so you can check it works - and load it up on the day, so it's all ready to go. 
  
- The best demos have someone 'driving' the demo, while someone else explains it, so the speaker can focus on the words/audience.  This does need reheasing, but it's always impressive as it demonstrates good team work.
  
- If you hve a web app, give us the url or a QR code so we can look at it while we listen.
  

If your project is a continuation of previous years
--
- Early in the presentation, tell us what the previous team did, and show us screenshots, gifs etc of what it was previously - make it very clear what work your team did.


Showcasing team working
--
- Make sure you know what every team mate's contributions were.  The best vivas had it in the presentations already, but we are always really impressed when team mates promote each other.  Don't lie of course.
  
- If you had team problems that got better, tell us how you changed it and what you did.
  
- If you did any role swapping, or rotating scrum managing etc, tell us - and tell us how taking on different roles changed how you worked.
