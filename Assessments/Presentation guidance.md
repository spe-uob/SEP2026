# Viva presentation

There will be two formative (unmarked) viva sessions in Weeks 10 to showcase the MVP and 19 for the Beta Release.  The final viva is summative (marked) and wil take place during the summer exam period (4-22 May 2026).

The **formative** vivas are opportunities for you to practice and get feedback. Formative vivas are sessions of 5-6 teams presenting in front of each other, staff and mentors, so you can see what other groups are doing.  Each formative viva is 20 minutes - 10 minutes for the Project demo and 10 minutes for questions. 

**Watching other teams' vivas** is important, as it's an opportunity to learn from each other, ask questions and get inspiration for your next viva. Make notes of things the other teams do well, so you can remember them for next time. 

The **final summative viva** will be just your team and a panel of staff markers.  Because you will have finished your project, this will be 25 minutes long - 15 minutes for the presentation and 10 minutes for questions.   

**Every team member should be present for the viva** - if you are ill or have transport issues etc, [email Sarah Connolly](mailto:sarah.connolly@bristol.ac.uk) as soon as you can.

**Ideally every team member should take part in the presentation and answer questions**. We know people can find speaking in public difficult, so in the formative vivas, if 1 member of the team doesn't present, this is fine - [email Sarah Connolly](mailto:sarah.connolly@bristol.ac.uk) in advance if you have difficulties that will prevent you speaking. If you have a Study Support Plan, we will write to you before the vivas to give you more opportunities to demonstrate your learning.

## What to include in the presentation

The presentation should be focused on the following:

- A very brief demonstration (2-3 minutes) of an operational version of the system, which we will be able to investigate and explore during the viva. This should showcase the important parts of the system (don't waste time showing us the login page!) 
- a set of slides to support the presentation. These should be uploaded to Blackboard as a PDF. 

In the session you will be expected to present both the finished product and the development process. 

We recommend that your presentation covers the following.  We won't expect you to have done everything in the MVP and Beta Release vivas, so be honest with us about where you are. 

- **Who you are:** Tell us your names, so we know who's talking!

- **Context:** 1 sentence about your client: who are they are, what is their business problem? 

- **Vision:** 1 sentence about what the project trying to achieve? What value does it bring? This can be in the [elevator pitch format](https://www.fsb.org.uk/resources-page/do-you-have-the-perfect-elevator-pitch.html) - this should be short and memorable.

- **User stories/requirements**: 1 or 2 key [user stories](https://github.com/spe-uob/SEP2025/tree/main/Assessments/Presentation%20examples%20-%202024-5) and requirements - very briefly describe these and tell us the extend to which these were implemented

- **Demo**: Ideally structured around the user stories.  If it is an app, you can borrow phones from the Tech Hun to show it on; if it's a website, include the address, so markers can look around it. 

- **Architecture diagram and description**: Include a high-level architecture diagram and tell us what tech stack did you use? What are your security and storage options? Remember a picture is worth 1000 words, so you don't need to talk about this if you have a good diagram

- **Development:** Summarise your overall test strategy - what % of code coverage do you have? Tell us about your Continuous Integration and Continuous Deployment (in the formative vivas, this might be your plans!)

- **User testing:**  Explain what testing processes you carried out and how it influenced you (in the MVP viva this might be your plans for future user testing)

- **Interface Design:** Summarise your approach to interface design, optionally illustrating it with original design wireframes & screenshots of current interfaces (again, remember a picture is worth 1000 words!)

- **Acceptance testing/ evaluation /feedback:** Tell us about any client feedback and user feedback of the late releases of the project

- **Project Management**: briefly tell us your approach and tools.  Include strategies and mechanisms put in place to ensure that your development process is mature and repeatable; e.g. git source control; meeting and collaboration culture and techniques; evaluation approach.  Are you using Agile?  How many sprints have you done?

- **Key challenges and problems that your team encountered (including technological, client-facing, groupwork etc.)** and how you solved them

**In the formative vivas**, please include a brief plan to get to the next release - what are the priorities? How will you work differently?

**For the summative viva**, include:
- **What you would do differently next time**, if you had the chance to undertake the project again

- **What you have learned** from the whole experience of undertaking this unit

> [!TIP]
> * Before your viva, check that your demos etc will work in the building you're presenting in.
>
> * Make sure everything is loaded, working and open on more than 1 laptop before the session begins, so you can start straight away and look professional. We understand that tech issues happen, but if another team member can jump in and take over, we will be impressed. 
>
> * Rehearse your presentation before you give it to check how long it takes and that you can transition easily between presenters.
>
> * Don’t be afraid to tell us you started slowly, didn’t work well or could have done more in TB1 – if you can turn that around in TB2 and explain what you learned and what you did differently, it will give you more marks than if you try to pretend everything went smoothly
>
> * Be careful about blaming other deadlines for not doing much work on SEP – every team has the same deadlines (except the Maths & CS students!) so if other groups are doing more than you this will backfire on you.
>
> * Keep the number of words low on your slides - think about the lecturers you like the best, and how they use slides versus words to get their meaning across.
>   
> * If it's a web app, give us the address so we can look at it! If it's a phone app, borrow phones from the Tech Hub to load it up so we can play with it!

> We don't recommend using ChatGPT or LLM tools for writing slides, as they tend to be overly wordy, and when you're reading words you didn't write, it comes across.

# SEP marking: Individual Marks
We will weight the group mark in order to arrive at individual marks for each member of the team. We will take into account the contributions that you have allocated to group members, as well as our own impression of individual performances within the group. We will also take into account feedback from the mentors who are helping to guide your project work.
